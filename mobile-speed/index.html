What slows online stores down on mobile

On 25 November 2026 I ran Google PageSpeed Insights (mobile) on 40 WooCommerce stores picked at random from the kainos.lt store list, a Lithuanian price comparison site. No store names, only the summary.

Key numbers

11 of 27stores with Google real-user data are above the recommended 2.5 s Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) across the whole site. Real-user LCP median 2.0 s.
36 of 40stores load CSS or JavaScript files that block rendering.
4.5 smedian LCP in the simulated PageSpeed mobile test (slow 4G). 28 of 40 were above 2.5 s in it.
1.8 MBmedian home page size; median image weight 0.4 MB. 11 of 40 pages weigh more than 3 MB.
7 of 40scored 90 or more in PageSpeed (median 75).

Five findings

1. Styles and scripts block rendering

36 of 40 stores load CSS or JavaScript files that stop the page from showing until they finish. PageSpeed estimates the first content would appear 1.4 s sooner in the test (median) if they were fixed, and more than 1 s sooner in 27 stores.

2. The main image starts loading too late

In 31 of 40 stores the largest element on the phone screen is an image. In 15 of them the browser started loading that image more than 1 s after the first server response. In 6 stores it is marked lazy, so the browser starts loading it later than it could.

3. Unused JavaScript

15 of 40 stores send phones more than 200 KB of JavaScript that is not used while the page loads. On WooCommerce these are often plugins that load their files on every page.

4. Oversized images

In most stores images are not the biggest problem, but PageSpeed estimates 8 of 40 would save more than 500 KB by compressing images, serving WebP or AVIF and sending phones a smaller size.

5. Short browser cache lifetimes

PageSpeed estimates 7 of 40 stores would save returning visitors more than 500 KB if the server let browsers keep files longer.

How to check your own store in 5 minutes

  1. Open pagespeed.web.dev, enter your home page URL and choose the Mobile tab.
  2. In the real-user section at the top, look at LCP. Choose Origin to see the whole site. If it is above 2.5 s, at least 25% of visits have an LCP above the recommended limit.
  3. Further down, under Insights, look for “Render blocking requests” and “Improve image delivery”. They list the exact files.

If the real-user section is missing, Google does not have enough data for your site yet.

How I measured

The kainos.lt list has 1240 stores. I shuffled it at random and checked home pages in that order until I found 40 WooCommerce stores (133 checked, 123 reachable). I detected WooCommerce from the page source, so some such stores may have been missed. No store was assessed in advance. One of the 40 is a Latvian store from the same list. This is a sample of stores listed on kainos.lt, not of all Lithuanian online stores.

Each store was measured once, on 25 November 2026, with the Google PageSpeed Insights API in mobile mode. The test simulates an average phone on a slower mobile connection, so its LCP is usually higher than what real visitors see (true for 25 of the 27 stores where I have both numbers).

Real-user data (Google CrUX, whole-site 75th percentile, phones, last 28 days) exists for 27 of 40 stores. Google has none for sites with less traffic.

Sizes are bytes transferred on load of the home page without scrolling, 1 MB = 1024 KB. Savings are PageSpeed estimates, not guaranteed results.
